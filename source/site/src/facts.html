The facts

Three facts about federal unions that the talking points leave out

Membership is voluntary. Pay and benefits are set by Congress, not at the bargaining table. And the government's own numbers show the system is cheap to run. Each one comes from the statute or from a government report, linked under it.

Fact 01

Nobody has to join, and nobody has to pay

The federal government has no closed shop, no agency fee and no “fair share” payment. It never has. An employee can spend a whole career in a union-represented job without joining or paying a cent, and the union still has to represent that employee.[[fn:crs44794]]

What the law says

Every employee has the right to join a union “or to refrain from any such activity, freely and without fear of penalty or reprisal.” 5 U.S.C. 7102[[fn:usc7102]]

The union must represent everyone in the unit “without discrimination and without regard to labor organization membership.” 5 U.S.C. 7114(a)(1)[[fn:usc7114]]

Dues come out of a paycheck only when the employee signs a written authorization. 5 U.S.C. 7115[[fn:usc7115]]

That is why official time exists. A union that must represent non-members for free, and may not charge them, was given paid time to do it. AFGE, the largest federal union, says more than half of the employees it represents pay no dues.[[fn:afgeff]]

Fact 02

Federal unions do not bargain pay or benefits

Congress sets federal pay, health insurance, retirement and leave by statute. The bargaining law takes anything “specifically provided for by Federal statute” off the table. What is left is working conditions: schedules, safety, fair procedures and how a change gets carried out.[[fn:crs44794]]

What the law says

“Conditions of employment” do not include matters set by federal statute. 5 U.S.C. 7103(a)(14)(C)[[fn:usc7103]]

Mission, budget, staffing levels, hiring, assignments and discipline are reserved to management. 5 U.S.C. 7106[[fn:usc7106]]

Striking is an unfair labor practice and grounds for removal. 5 U.S.C. 7116(b)(7)[[fn:usc7116]] and 7311[[fn:usc7311]]

The Congressional Research Service puts it in one line: “Most federal employees cannot bargain over wages or benefits.”[[fn:crs42526]] The other side agrees. A Heritage Foundation analyst wrote in 2025 that federal unions are, with few exceptions, “prohibited from bargaining for pay and most benefits.”[[fn:heritage25]]

The exceptions are small and Congress wrote each one. About seven agencies bargain pay under their own statutes, and the Postal Service bargains pay under a separate law.[[fn:crs44794]]

Fact 03

Bargaining is cheap, and where the savings were measured it paid for itself

The main taxpayer cost of federal bargaining is official time: paid hours employees spend representing coworkers in grievances and negotiations. In fiscal 2019 that came to 2.6 million hours and about $135 million across the government, or less than two hours a year for each represented employee. It is about one-tenth of one percent of federal pay and benefits.[[fn:opm19,fedsmith19,fedweek]]

What the record shows

An independent Booz Allen analysis of the Customs Service partnership with its union found about $1.25 back for every dollar spent, and $3 million in net savings from 1993 to 1998. Senate Hearing 112-417[[fn:hearing112]]

The labor board reported that cases settled without litigation rose 47 percent under partnership. Kelly Air Force Base alone avoided $2 million in litigation costs, and NASA projected $40 million in savings from changes its labor-management groups proposed. Government Executive, 1996[[fn:ge96]]

A peer-reviewed study of federal partnerships found they reduced labor-management disputes. Industrial and Labor Relations Review, 2006[[fn:ilr06]]

Conflict is the expensive part. GAO estimated that processing 6,448 unfair labor practice charges in one year cost about $25.9 million. GAO, 1982[[fn:gao82]]

The clearest current example is in the National Guard. The Pentagon's own estimate says replacing union-eligible technicians with military personnel will cost about $248 million more over five years.[[fn:lp291,crs13300]] See the National Guard page.

What we could not findNo CBO, CRS or RAND study puts a single dollar figure on the net savings from federal bargaining, and we will not invent one. The studies that find public unions raise costs are studies of state and local governments, where unions bargain pay and benefits. One of the best known found the effect was largest for benefits. Federal unions bargain neither. Anzia and Moe[[fn:anzia]]

National Guard technicians

The Guard's full-time civilians, and the plan to replace them with soldiers who cannot bargain

LIUNA Local 1776 represents National Guard technicians and Title 5 employees. They are a category of federal employee found nowhere else, and for fifty years state adjutants general have tried to get them out from under the bargaining law. In 2026 the Pentagon asked Congress to end the technician program altogether.

    The AGR conversion

    What the plan does

    On May 11, 2026, the Defense Department sent Congress a proposal to phase out dual-status technicians over ten years. The House wrote it into its defense bill as section 1105. The Senate committee's bill has a similar section 513.[[fn:crs13300,sasc]]

    • No new technicians may be hired starting October 1, 2028.
    • Technician positions convert to AGR or to Title 5 civilian positions. The stated end state is 89 percent AGR and 11 percent civilian.
    • An occupied position converts only with the employee's written consent.
    • Section 709 of Title 32 is repealed on October 1, 2038. After that date no technician can be paid.

    The Department's stated reasons are readiness and workforce management. Its proposal says dual status is a burden on the technician, that career paths are rigid and that running two personnel systems is complicated. A National Guard Bureau paper adds that hiring a technician takes about 90 days against 30 for an AGR.[[fn:lp291]] The adjutants general, the National Guard Association and the enlisted association support it.

    How we got there

    In June 2026 Local 1776 gave Congress a 44-page cost analysis with the spreadsheet behind it.[[fn:l1776]] The model is simple on purpose so anyone can check it.

    • The baseline is the law. The fiscal 2026 defense act authorizes 31,699 technicians and 56,827 AGRs in the Army and Air Guard.
    • The conversion is the Pentagon's. At 89 percent, about 28,212 technician positions become AGR and 3,487 become Title 5.
    • The premium is modest. $10,000 more per AGR position per year in the conservative scenario, $16,000 when allowances, health care and retirement are loaded in. Title 5 conversions are counted as costing nothing extra.
    • What is left out makes the number low. No transition costs, no retraining, no lawsuits, no lost state contributions, no loss of experience.

    These are our benchmarks, built from published Defense Department, CBO and CNA findings. They are not an official score. We have not found one from the Congressional Budget Office or GAO, and Congress should not vote until it has both.

    It was also the answer in 2017

    The Institute for Defense Analyses studied the question for the Pentagon at Congress's direction. It found that replacing technicians with AGRs costs the same or more in every reserve component.[[fn:ida]]

    For the Army National Guard, converting every technician to a mix of 80 percent AGR and 20 percent civilian added about $398 million a year in program cost and $762 million a year when retirement and other long-term costs were counted. For the Air National Guard the figures were $156 million and $490 million.[[fn:ida]]

    Fifty years of studies, and none found savings

    • 1968Congress considered putting technicians in military status when it wrote the Technicians Act and rejected it. The Senate report said that approach “would involve considerably more cost.”[[fn:l1776]] Source pending
    • 1978The Congressional Budget Office found Guard and Reserve forces cost about one-fifth as much as active forces, person for person.[[fn:l1776]] Source pending
    • 1980After the Army ran a four-year test of replacing technicians with full-time military, GAO found no evidence it improved readiness.[[fn:l1776]] Source pending
    • 2013The Pentagon's own cost office found a drilling Guard member costs about 85 percent less than an active-duty counterpart. A CNA study the same year found AGR retirement costs more per person than technician retirement.[[fn:l1776]] Source pending
    • 2017The Institute for Defense Analyses, working for the Pentagon, found the same or higher cost in every component when AGRs replace technicians.[[fn:ida]]
    • 2024The Congressional Budget Office again listed the opposite move as a way to save money: replacing 80,000 military personnel in support jobs with 64,000 civilians would save about $17 billion over ten years.[[fn:cbo24]]
    • 2026The Pentagon's own table for this proposal shows a net cost increase of $248 million in the first five years.[[fn:lp291]]

    It ends bargaining for good

    An executive order can be cancelled by the next president. A converted position cannot bargain under any president.

    AGR members serve on “full-time National Guard duty.” Federal law makes it a crime to enroll them in a union or to bargain on their behalf.[[fn:usc976]] Chapter 71 separately excludes members of the uniformed services from the definition of employee.[[fn:usc7103]]

    Bargaining is not all that goes. A technician can take a firing to the Merit Systems Protection Board and a discrimination complaint to the EEOC, with a union representative alongside.[[fn:mspb]] An AGR member has military channels, and the proposal offers nothing to replace what is lost.

    It fixes nothing

    The Department has not named a job an AGR can do that a technician cannot. The proposal shows the opposite. One of its own subsections has to amend 32 U.S.C. 328(b) so that AGR members are allowed to do the maintenance and repair work technicians already do.[[fn:lp291]]

    The work does not change. The equipment does not change. What changes is who does it, what it costs and whether the people doing it have any rights on the job.

    Why we say ending bargaining is one of the drivers

    The proposal never mentions unions. The Department says this is about readiness. Here is the record that leads us to a different conclusion.

    • The adjutants general asked Congress to ban technician unions in 1978.Fifty-three of them, through the National Guard Association, asked that technicians be removed from the bargaining program. Congress refused.[[fn:brief]]
    • The Guard Bureau tried three times in two years to pull technicians out of the civilian discrimination complaint process.In 2013 by internal direction, in the fiscal 2014 defense bill, and in a 2015 reorganization plan, it sought to move technician complaints into military channels with no union representative. LIUNA and the Association of Civilian Technicians opposed each one and Congress wrote the rights into section 709 instead.[[fn:l1776]] Source pending
    • Ohio's adjutant general walked away from a 45-year bargaining relationship in 2016.The Ohio Guard declared the bargaining law did not apply to technicians and shut off dues. It lost at the labor board, at the Sixth Circuit and at the Supreme Court, 7 to 2.[[fn:alj,scotus]]
    • The Pentagon's 2017 study treated technician bargaining as a problem.It warned that bargaining over military aspects of the program could hurt readiness, and it noted that AGRs cannot unionize.[[fn:ida]]
    • The Secretary has said where he stands.Asked by lawmakers in 2026 why he cancelled nearly every union contract in the Department, he said it “should not be subject to collective bargaining.”[[fn:mm26]]
    • Money is not the reason.By the Department's own estimate the conversion costs more. If savings were the goal, the civilian share would not be 11 percent.

    The alternative the Pentagon passed over

    If the goal is a simpler, more modern workforce, there is a way to get it that saves money and keeps every capability: convert technicians to Title 5 civilian positions. Congress did it for part of the workforce in 2017. LIUNA supports a full conversion.

    A Title 5 employee draws no housing or subsistence allowance, pays a share of health insurance and earns a civilian retirement. CBO's studies of replacing military personnel with civilians point the same way.[[fn:cbo24]] The Pentagon chose a workforce that is 89 percent military instead.

    What Congress should do

    • Strike section 1105 of H.R. 8800 and section 513 of S. 4784 from the final fiscal 2027 defense bill.
    • No phase-out without a CBO score and a GAO review. Both should compare the Pentagon's plan with the status quo and with a full Title 5 conversion, on the same assumptions.
    • Keep the rule against coercion. The fiscal 2026 defense act already bars a state from pressuring a technician into conversion or punishing one who declines.[[fn:sasc]]
    • Keep civilian protections in place. Bargaining, appeal and discrimination complaint rights should stay with the workforce through any transition.
    • Name technicians and Title 5 Guard employees in the rewrite of Chapter 71, so neither the national security exception nor a position conversion can be used to empty their bargaining units.

    Where it stands

    The House passed its defense bill on July 22, 2026, by 216 to 212, with the phaseout in it.[[fn:ndaa]] The Senate never passed its version. The four Armed Services leaders are expected to negotiate a final text in the lame-duck session, with a vote in December.

    Local 1776 gave the Senate committee its response in May and its cost analysis in June. AFGE and IFPTE have asked the negotiators to drop the phaseout.[[fn:afge26]] The decision will be made by the outgoing Congress, before the new majorities are seated on January 3.
